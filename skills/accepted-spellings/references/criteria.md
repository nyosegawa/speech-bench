# How to decide

The bench accepts a transcription that writes the sentence as written, writes any part in the kana of its
reading, writes a bracketed stretch as one of its other spellings, or leaves out an optional stretch. Two
mistakes are possible, and they are not equal:

- Accepting a spelling of a different word hides a real mishearing. Avoid this above all.
- Missing a spelling of the same words counts a correct transcription as wrong. This costs less.

## Readings

Give every part that is not kana the reading a person reading this sentence aloud would say, with the
sound changes it takes there (三本 is さんぼん, 3時頃 is さんじごろ). Read Latin letters and foreign words the
way a Japanese reader would say them. Where people commonly say a part two ways, give both: 良《よ／い》い.

Split a base as finely as its reading splits, so that a transcription mixing kanji and kana passes:
子《こ》供《ども》. Keep whole a reading that belongs to the word as a whole: 今日《きょう》.

## Other spellings

Add the other ways a Japanese writer would write the same words, where the readings do not give them:

- a number in kanji or in digits: ［九《く》時《じ》／9時］, ［｜120《ひゃくにじゅう》万《まん》／百二十万／1200000］;
- kanji for a word the sentence writes in kana, other okurigana, another katakana rendering, Latin letters
  for a katakana word: ［たとえば／例えば］, ［受《う》け付《つ》け／受付］, ［コンピューター／コンピュータ］,
  ［ズーム／Zoom］;
- the intended text where the sentence has a clear typo, with a note: ［以《い》外《がい》／意外］ in 以外な結果.

Never accept a different word: another kanji for the same sound (会う and 合う), another word with the same
meaning (4時半 and 4時30分), another spoken form (食べてる for 食べている). A numeral inside a word that is not
a number takes no digits (一緒, 十分 meaning enough).

Fillers (えー, えーと, あの, うーん, まあ and the like) may always be left out: ［えーと／えっと／］.

When unsure whether a spelling is the same words, leave it out and say why in the note.
